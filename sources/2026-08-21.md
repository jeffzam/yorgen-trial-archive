## Cross-check and attribution notes

- All four outlets agree that the defence requested dissolution of the jury trial because of possible headline exposure on court-issued tablets, that the prosecution objected, and that Judge Edwina Grima refused the request.
- The ruling is summarised narrowly: the judge found no concrete evidence that jurors accessed the identified headlines or articles and said jurors would be directed to decide only on admitted evidence. The archive does not treat potential visibility as proven exposure or proven prejudice.
- All four outlets report that Fenech denied ordering or commissioning the assassination. MaltaToday, Times of Malta and The Malta Independent provide the fuller afternoon account and agree that he acknowledged giving Theuma €50,000 and passing police information while offering exculpatory explanations.
- Fenech’s allegations concerning Schembri, Theuma, Vella, Cutajar, Brincat and others are reported testimony. Schembri, Theuma and Vella gave materially different prior accounts or denials. The archive keeps those versions separate and does not decide who is truthful.
- The alleged USB recordings mentioned by Fenech have not been produced in the trial reporting reviewed. Times of Malta notes that Theuma previously said no such recordings exist. The archive therefore labels the USB as alleged and unproduced.
- Destination wording in accounts of the Gozo episode is not uniform across the reports. The timeline says only that Fenech described a suggestion that he leave or disappear and does not silently reconcile the destination.
- A date reference in The Malta Independent’s live report concerning an earlier raid differs from the chronology in other reporting. The archive does not use that disputed date.
- The court recording of Michelle Camilleri’s site-visit remarks was checked after the defence requested clarification. The archive records the procedural exchange without suggesting that it resolved the wider evidentiary dispute.
- Fenech has pleaded not guilty and is presumed innocent. His testimony is evidence for the jury to assess, not a judicial finding.
